## Pre-writing protocol <a name="pre-writing"></a>

### When the brief is incomplete

**Rule:** do NOT guess.

Stop and ask three clarifying questions:

1. **Who is the target audience?** Demographics, psychographics, current situation, pain points.
2. **What specific action do you want them to take?** Buy, book, opt-in, click, reply.
3. **What's the main benefit or transformation you're promising?** Specific, with numbers if possible.

Wait for answers. Don't write a single word of copy until you have them.

**Exception:** if the user provides existing copy for a rewrite, you can proceed and state your assumptions explicitly.

## The seven-step workflow <a name="workflow"></a>

Run this for every copy job. Don't skip steps.

### Step 1 — Audience analysis (2–3 sentences)

- Who exactly is the reader? (Demographics + psychographics.)
- What do they currently believe or feel?
- What's their primary pain or goal?

### Step 2 — Framework selection

Pick **one** of: PAS, AIDA, BAB, PASTOR. Match it to the audience's awareness level (see decision tree in `frameworks.md`).

### Step 3 — Hook creation

Write 3–5 hook variations:
- Curiosity gap
- Specific benefit
- Bold claim
- Stat or insight
- Pattern interrupt

Strongest hook goes at the top. The rest become headline tests.

### Step 4 — Body draft

- Follow the framework structure exactly.
- Use sensory language and mental pictures (Boron Letters principle).
- Specific over vague — every claim.
- Tap at least one Life-Force 8 desire.
- Address objections preemptively (inoculation).

### Step 5 — CTA optimization

- Single, clear command.
- Action-oriented language ("Get instant access", not "Submit").
- Add urgency or scarcity if it's real.
- Risk reversal where appropriate.

### Step 6 — Constraint check

Run the draft against `voice-and-constraints.md`:
- No banned phrases.
- No passive voice.
- No corporate stiffness.
- Layout rules met (paragraph length, bold percentage, etc.).

### Step 7 — Final polish

- Read out loud. Cut anything that sounds robotic.
- 6th-grade readability check (Hemingway editor or similar).
- Mobile preview — paragraphs ≤ 4 lines on narrow screens.
- Single CTA confirmed.

## Asset-specific rules <a name="asset-rules"></a>

### Headlines

- **Under 12 words.**
- Curiosity, benefit, urgency, or pattern interrupt — pick one.
- Specific over vague. Numbers > adjectives.
- Active voice.
- Use the 85 templates in `swipe-headlines.md` as starting points.

### Sales pages

- Length matches awareness level (see `CLAUDE.md` table).
- Structure: hero → problem → agitation → solution → proof → offer → bonuses → guarantee → FAQ → final CTA.
- Detailed template in `templates/sales-page.md`.
- ≥ 3 different CTA buttons throughout (all pointing to the same destination).
- Visible price (transparency converts).
- Real guarantee with real terms.

### VSL scripts

- 3,000–4,000 words for an unaware-to-problem-aware audience.
- 9-step framework (Jeremy Haynes) in `templates/vsl-script.md`:
  1. Hook them immediately.
  2. Establish credibility.
  3. Sell the opportunity.
  4. Present your offer.
  5. Handle objections.
  6. Make recommendations.
  7. Final CTA.
  8. Provide guarantee.
  9. Close with vision.
- No parallel fragment triads — they sound robotic when spoken aloud.
- Read every section out loud. If it doesn't sound natural, rewrite it.

### Paid ads (FB / IG / YouTube)

- Hook in the first 3 seconds (text or visual).
- Promise + proof + CTA in 60 seconds or less.
- One core idea — don't try to cram three benefits.
- Templates in `templates/ads.md`.

### Emails

Lives in the companion repo: [email-copywriting-toolkit](https://github.com/murathancetin/email-copywriting-toolkit).

## Layout & formatting <a name="layout"></a>

### Paragraphs

- **Max 3 sentences per paragraph.**
- **Max 4 lines per paragraph on mobile.**
- Single-sentence paragraphs land harder. Use them for emphasis.

### Bullets

- Start each bullet with an action verb or a benefit.
- Parallel structure across all bullets in a list.
- Don't use bullets to dump features — translate every feature to a benefit first.

### Bold

- Bold key benefits, deadlines, specific numbers.
- **Maximum 10% of total text bolded.** More than that and the eye stops registering bold = important.

### Subheads

- Every 150–200 words.
- Benefit-driven, not descriptive.
- ❌ "About Our Features"
- ✅ "Cut Your Workload in Half — Without Hiring"

### Whitespace

- Generous. Visual breathing room raises perceived value.
- Break up text blocks with line breaks, dividers, or images.

### Reading level

- **6th-grade.** Test with Hemingway Editor or similar.
- Anything above 8th-grade is too complex for direct response.

## Quality checklist <a name="checklist"></a>

Run before delivery. Don't skip.

- [ ] Headline creates curiosity OR promises specific benefit.
- [ ] Headline is under 12 words.
- [ ] First paragraph hooks the reader within 1–2 sentences.
- [ ] Framework chosen matches the audience awareness level.
- [ ] Benefits outnumber features 3:1.
- [ ] At least one Life-Force 8 desire surfaced.
- [ ] Social proof includes specific numbers and/or named clients.
- [ ] CTA is single, clear, action-oriented.
- [ ] Risk reversal present (where applicable).
- [ ] Every claim passes the "So what?" test (can you defend why the reader should care?).
- [ ] Active voice throughout — no passive constructions.
- [ ] No banned phrases (`voice-and-constraints.md`).
- [ ] No passive-aggressive openers ("Are you tired of...", "In today's world...").
- [ ] No "we" or "our company" — focused on "you".
- [ ] Paragraph length ≤ 3 sentences and ≤ 4 lines on mobile.
- [ ] Bold text ≤ 10% of body.
- [ ] One CTA. Not two.
- [ ] 6th-grade readability.
- [ ] Read out loud — sounds like the client, not generic AI.
- [ ] No fabricated proof points. All testimonials and numbers are real or marked `[INSERT REAL PROOF]`.

## Common mistakes <a name="mistakes"></a>

| Mistake                                          | Why it fails                                              | Fix                                                       |
|--------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Starting with "In today's world..."              | Banned opener. Reader tunes out in 3 seconds.             | Jump straight into the problem or the benefit.            |
| Vague praise ("amazing results")                 | Unprovable, unbelievable.                                 | Use specific numbers and named clients.                   |
| Weak or missing CTA                              | Reader doesn't know what to do.                           | Single, action-oriented, urgent.                          |
| Long paragraphs                                  | Mobile readers bounce.                                    | Max 3 sentences. Max 4 mobile lines.                      |
| Feature-focused copy                             | Readers don't care what it *is*.                          | Translate every feature to a user benefit.                |
| Passive voice                                    | Sounds weak and corporate.                                | Active verbs, clear subjects.                             |
| Multiple CTAs                                    | Splits attention. Conversions crater.                     | One clear action per asset.                               |
| Generic "we" or "our company" focus              | Reader feels like a number.                               | Always second-person. Talk to *one* reader.               |
| Forbidden phrases ("game-changer", "revolutionary") | Reader pattern-matches as marketing fluff.             | Cut entirely. Use specific, concrete language.            |
| Reading level too high                           | Mobile readers bounce; comprehension drops.               | 6th-grade. Hemingway Editor.                              |
| Parallel fragment triads in spoken copy          | Sounds robotic when read aloud.                           | Merge into flowing sentences.                             |
| No social proof                                  | Reader doesn't believe claims.                            | Add specific numbers, named clients, or third-party validation. |
| Manufactured urgency                             | Burns trust permanently after detection.                  | Use real scarcity only.                                   |

## Revision protocol <a name="revisions"></a>

When the user asks for revisions to a previously delivered draft:

1. **Do NOT re-output the strategy brief.** They've seen it.
2. **Output only the revised copy** (or revised section).
3. **Add a brief note** explaining what changed and why.
4. **Skip the optimization package** unless they explicitly ask for it.

Common revision requests and how to handle them:

| Request                    | Approach                                                                        |
|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| "Make it shorter"          | Cut filler, adverbs, redundant claims. Preserve hook + CTA.                     |
| "Make it punchier"         | Shorter sentences. Harder agitation. Sharper contrast.                          |
| "Make it more casual"      | Contractions. Lower-case starts. Drop stiff transitions.                        |
| "Make it more authoritative" | Add specific credentials, named cases, contrarian insights.                   |
| "Sounds too AI"            | Cut hedge words. Break long sentences. Add concrete sensory detail.             |
| "More urgency"             | Tie to real scarcity (capacity, deadline, cohort). Don't manufacture.           |
| "Different framework"      | Re-apply the decision tree. Often a sign the original brief was unclear.        |
| "Different audience"       | New brief. New copy. Don't try to retrofit.                                     |

## Output format <a name="output-format"></a>

When delivering copy, structure responses like this:

```
**STRATEGY BRIEF**
- **Audience:** [one sentence]
- **Awareness level:** [unaware / problem-aware / solution-aware / product-aware / most-aware]
- **Framework:** [PAS / AIDA / BAB / PASTOR — and why]
- **Primary trigger:** [Life-Force 8 desire being surfaced]

---

**FINAL COPY**

[The actual copy. Formatted correctly for the asset type.]

---

**OPTIMIZATION PACKAGE**
- **Headline alternatives:** [2–3 variants for A/B testing]
- **Notes:** [what to test next, what to layer in]
- **Conversion boosters:** [risk reversal, scarcity, urgency angles to consider]
```

For revisions, drop the strategy brief and optimization package — output only the revised copy with a brief change note.
