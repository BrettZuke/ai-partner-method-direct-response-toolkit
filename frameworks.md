# Frameworks

The four structural patterns that handle 95% of direct response copy. Pick one based on the audience's awareness level and the asset's goal — don't mix them in a single piece.

---

## The decision tree

Start here. Every copy job begins with: *what does the reader currently know?*

```
What does the reader know?

├── Doesn't know they have the problem (UNAWARE)
│   └── Use AIDA or PASTOR (long, story-driven, education-heavy)
│
├── Knows the pain, doesn't know solutions exist (PROBLEM-AWARE)
│   └── Use PAS (lead with their pain in their words)
│
├── Knows solutions exist, comparing options (SOLUTION-AWARE)
│   └── Use BAB or AIDA (differentiation + transformation)
│
├── Knows your specific product (PRODUCT-AWARE)
│   └── Use PAS short-form (handle objections, push past resistance)
│
└── Ready to buy, just needs a nudge (MOST-AWARE)
    └── Use PAS short-form (urgency, scarcity, clear CTA)
```

---

## PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

**Use when:** the audience is aware of their pain and looking for relief.

**Best for:** problem-aware and most-aware copy. Most landing pages. Most ads. Short emails.

### Structure

| Stage | Strategy                                            | Rules                                                              |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Problem** | State the customer's pain in their exact words. | Pull verbatim from reviews, surveys, or sales calls. Specific.     |
| **Agitate** | Amplify the emotional cost of not solving it.    | Use "What's worse..." or "Even more frustrating..." transitions.    |
| **Solution** | Position your offer as the inevitable fix.      | Use "That's exactly why..." transition. Single clear CTA.          |

### Example skeleton

> *Subject: Why your last 3 launches stalled at $8k*
>
> **(Problem)** You wrote good copy. You ran solid ads. The launch still flatlined at $8k and you don't know why.
>
> **(Agitate)** Every month it stays this way is another month watching peers double up while you're stuck refining the same funnel that isn't working. The ads work. The copy works. Yet nothing's moving.
>
> **(Solution)** The piece you're missing isn't ads or copy. It's a follow-up sequence that converts the 70% of leads who don't buy on day one. That's exactly what we map out on a strategy call.
>
> [CTA: Book your call]

### When *not* to use

- Cold audiences who don't know they have the problem yet → use AIDA.
- When you're selling a transformation more than fixing a pain → use BAB.

## AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Use when:** cold or semi-cold audience. They need to be hooked from scratch.

**Best for:** unaware audiences, cold ads, top-of-funnel content, awareness campaigns.

### Structure

| Stage | Strategy                                              | Rules                                                                   |
|-------|-------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Attention** | Hook the reader.                                | Under 12 words. Curiosity gap, bold claim, or surprising statistic.     |
| **Interest** | Bridge to the reader's world.                    | "Here's why..." or "The reason is...". Why this matters to *them*.       |
| **Desire** | Paint the transformation.                          | Use "Imagine..." scenarios. Include social proof.                        |
| **Action** | Move to conversion.                                | Single, clear command. Create urgency.                                  |

### Example skeleton

> *Subject: 73% of fitness coaches make under $4k/mo*
>
> **(Attention)** Three out of four coaches you follow on Instagram aren't breaking $4k a month. They're broke and playing the part.
>
> **(Interest)** Here's why: the coaching industry trains you to deliver — not to sell. So the people with the best programs end up worst at marketing.
>
> **(Desire)** Imagine running a coaching business where you wake up to 5 booked calls a week, all qualified, all ready to pay. No DM grind. No content treadmill. Just leads coming to you. That's exactly what the top 27% have built.
>
> **(Action)** Book a 30-minute call to see if this could work for your business: [Link]

### When *not* to use

- Hot audiences. They don't need the hook — they need the solution.
- When you have specific case study data — BAB tells the story better.

## BAB — Before, After, Bridge

**Use when:** selling a transformation. Especially good for testimonial-driven copy and case studies.

**Best for:** solution-aware audiences, transformation-focused offers, social proof emails.

### Structure

| Stage | Strategy                                       | Rules                                                                |
|-------|------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Before** | Current frustrating state.                | Sensory details. Emotional language. Specific situation.              |
| **After** | Desired outcome state.                     | Specific transformation. Numbers and timeframes if you have them.    |
| **Bridge** | Show the mechanism.                       | Explain "how" in simple terms. No jargon. Make it feel achievable.    |

### Example skeleton

> *Subject: From 0 clients to 12 in 90 days*
>
> **(Before)** Sarah was running a yoga studio that had been losing money for 18 months. She'd tried Facebook ads, Instagram, partnerships with local gyms — nothing stuck. She was three months from closing.
>
> **(After)** Today she has 12 monthly members, runs at 80% capacity, and is hiring her first instructor. Net revenue: $11k/mo.
>
> **(Bridge)** The change wasn't more marketing. It was a single referral system that turned every existing member into a recruiter — built in one weekend. We can map the same system for your studio in 30 minutes.
>
> [CTA: see if it works for your studio]

### When *not* to use

- When you don't have a real before/after story. Don't fake it. Use AIDA or PAS instead.

## PASTOR — Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response

**Use when:** long-form sales pages or VSLs for high-ticket offers. You have time and space to build belief.

**Best for:** sales pages 3,000+ words, VSL scripts 15+ minutes, high-ticket coaching offers.

### Structure

| Stage | Strategy                                          | Rules                                                                  |
|-------|---------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Problem** | Identify the core frustration.              | Lead with it. First sentence or first paragraph.                       |
| **Amplify** | Make the pain feel urgent.                  | Show the cost of ignoring the problem. Stack consequences.             |
| **Story** | Include relatability.                          | 2–3 sentence case study or founder story. Real names, real numbers.    |
| **Transformation** | Show the specific outcome.            | Numbers. Timeframes. Concrete picture of life after.                   |
| **Offer** | Present the value proposition.                 | Include risk reversal. Make the offer feel undeniable.                 |
| **Response** | Single call to action.                      | One clear next step with deadline or scarcity.                          |

### Example structure (sales page)

```
[HEADLINE — Problem hook in 12 words or fewer]

(Problem)
For 3 paragraphs: identify the exact pain in the audience's words.

(Amplify)
For 3-5 paragraphs: show what continuing to live with this pain looks like
in 6 months, 1 year, 5 years. Stack costs — financial, emotional, opportunity.

(Story)
For 4-6 paragraphs: founder story OR a case study of a client who was
exactly where the reader is now. Specifics, names, numbers.

(Transformation)
For 4-6 paragraphs: paint the desired outcome. Sensory details. What does
their life look like, feel like, sound like after?

(Offer)
For 3-5 paragraphs: what's included. Real price. Risk reversal. Bonuses
that solve specific objections.

(Response)
For 1-2 paragraphs: single CTA. Real urgency or scarcity if you have it.
[CTA Button]
```

### When *not* to use

- Short-form contexts (ads, emails, headlines). PASTOR needs space to build belief.
- Most-aware audiences who just need the offer and CTA.

## Framework selection by asset type

What works most often for each asset:

| Asset                          | Default framework  | Notes                                                          |
|--------------------------------|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Cold ad (FB/IG/YouTube)        | AIDA               | Hook in 3 seconds. Short.                                      |
| Retargeting ad                 | PAS                | They've seen you. Push past resistance.                        |
| Landing page (opt-in)          | AIDA short-form    | One promise, single field, single CTA.                         |
| Sales page (mid-ticket)        | PAS or BAB         | Match to awareness level.                                      |
| Sales page (high-ticket)       | PASTOR             | Long-form, story-driven, full belief-build.                    |
| VSL script (cold traffic)      | PASTOR             | Story-driven, education-heavy, 20+ minutes.                    |
| VSL script (warm traffic)      | PAS or BAB         | Shorter. Faster to the offer.                                  |
| Email subject line             | Curiosity / benefit / urgency model (3 patterns)        |
| Email body                     | PAS most often. BAB for case studies. AIDA for cold lists. |
| Headline                       | One of 85 templates in `swipe-headlines.md`             |

## What changes by framework — what doesn't

**Stays the same regardless of framework:**

- Single CTA per asset.
- Active voice.
- 6th-grade readability.
- Mobile-friendly formatting (≤4 lines per paragraph).
- At least one credibility element (real testimonial, specific number, named client).
- No forbidden phrases.

**Changes by framework:**

- Opening line (PAS = pain, AIDA = hook, BAB = scene-setting, PASTOR = pain stated then amplified).
- Order of emotional beats.
- Length (PASTOR is longest, PAS varies, AIDA varies, BAB usually mid-length).
- How long until you mention the offer (PASTOR: late. PAS: middle. AIDA: late. BAB: middle/late).

## Framework misuse — common errors

| Error                                                          | Fix                                                       |
|----------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Using PAS on a cold list                                        | They don't know the pain yet. Switch to AIDA.             |
| BAB without a real before/after story                          | Don't fabricate. Switch to PAS or AIDA.                   |
| PASTOR on a 200-word ad                                         | Too long for the format. Use AIDA or PAS.                 |
| Mixing AIDA and PAS in the same piece                          | Pick one. Mixing dilutes both.                            |
| AIDA hook that doesn't bridge to interest                      | The "A" hooked them but the "I" didn't bring them home. Rewrite the second paragraph. |
| Generic broadcast when you have segmentation data              | Pick the framework, then customize the hook by segment.   |
| Writing the framework first, the audience later                | Audience first, always. Framework follows from awareness level. |
