# VSL Script Template (9-Step Framework)

**Goal:** A video sales letter script that hooks attention, builds belief, and closes the sale.

**Recommended length:**

| Audience awareness   | VSL length      |
|----------------------|------------------|
| Unaware              | 20–45 minutes    |
| Problem-aware        | 15–30 minutes    |
| Solution-aware       | 10–20 minutes    |
| Product-aware        | 5–10 minutes     |
| Most-aware           | 2–5 minutes      |

**Word count rule of thumb:** ~150 words per minute of spoken video. So a 20-minute VSL = ~3,000 words.

---

## Pre-writing — VSL research survey

**RULE:** Complete this before writing a single word. If your script doesn't reflect what's in this table, you're not writing strategically — you're guessing.

| Field | Your answer |
|-------|-------------|
| **Goal** | (1–2 sentences — what is this VSL trying to achieve?) |
| **Primary audience** | Demographics, psychographics, current situation |
| **Awareness level** | Unaware / Problem-aware / Solution-aware / Product-aware / Most-aware |
| **Core pain points** | Top 3 frustrations the audience faces |
| **Top objections** | 3 reasons they wouldn't buy |
| **Unique mechanism** | What makes this offer different or better |
| **Swipe inspiration** | 3 proven hooks, headlines, or angles to model |
| **Emotional frame** | Fear, greed, curiosity, betrayal, etc. |
| **Logical frame** | ROI, speed, automation, etc. |
| **Urgency / scarcity** | Why should they act now? |
| **Primary CTA** | The one action you want them to take |
| **Visual concepts** | What visuals or scenes will help paint the picture? |

## The 9-step framework (Jeremy Haynes)

Run these in order. Don't skip steps.

---

### Step 1 — Hook them immediately (15–60 seconds)

Tell them exactly what you can do for them. No fluff.

```
[OPENING LINE — direct hook]
"Hey, my name is [Name]. I help [audience] [specific outcome]. If you're looking to [desire], I'm your [person/company/etc.]."

[OPTIONAL: pattern interrupt or contrarian claim]
"In the next [X] minutes, I'm going to show you exactly how [specific outcome] — and why most of what you've been told about [topic] is keeping you stuck."

[BRIDGE TO NEXT STEP]
"Stick with me through this video and I promise it'll be worth your time. Let me explain why."
```

**Rules:**
- Tell them who you are.
- Tell them what you do for them.
- Make them think "I'm in the right place."
- No "Hey guys what's up YouTube" energy.

---

### Step 2 — Establish credibility (1–2 minutes)

Build trust by showing WHY you can help them.

```
[CREDIBILITY PARAGRAPH]
"Over the last [X] years, I've worked with [specific number] [audience type], helping them [specific outcome with numbers]. My clients have [aggregate result]. I've personally [achieved specific milestone]."

[OPTIONAL: borrowed authority]
"You may have seen my work in [publication / podcast / association]. I've been featured by [credible source]."

[OPTIONAL: relevant story — 30–60 seconds]
"And the reason I'm so committed to this work is [brief origin story — connects to the audience's pain]."
```

**Rules:**
- Specific numbers only. No "lots of clients" or "many years."
- Only credentials that directly relate to how you help them.
- No bragging tone. Let the proof speak.

---

### Step 3 — Sell the opportunity (2–4 minutes)

Broaden the scope. Sell the bigger "why" behind your offer — the industry, the moment, the trend.

```
[SET THE STAGE]
"Right now, [industry/space/moment] is at a turning point. [Specific change happening]. The people who [adapt/learn/do X] are going to [win]. The people who don't are going to [lose]."

[PAINT THE OPPORTUNITY]
"Here's what's happening in [space]:
[Trend 1 with data]
[Trend 2 with data]
[Trend 3 with data]"

[CONTRAST]
"And here's why most [audience] are missing it: [the misconception that's keeping them stuck]."

[BRIDGE TO OFFER]
"That's exactly why I built [program/system/solution] — so [audience] can [seize the opportunity] without [common obstacle]."
```

**Rules:**
- Use real data and trends.
- Make them see the cost of doing nothing.
- Build belief that the opportunity is real and timely.

---

### Step 4 — Present your offer (3–5 minutes)

Transition into HOW you can specifically help.

```
[OFFER INTRODUCTION]
"So here's how I want to help you take advantage of this. My [Program Name] is a [duration] [type — coaching / course / done-for-you / etc.] designed to [specific outcome]."

[PRICE — STATED UPFRONT]
"It costs [$X] [or 3 payments of $Y]. And here's exactly what comes with it."

[COMPONENT BREAKDOWN — 30–60 seconds each]

"First, you get [Component 1]. This is [description]. The reason this matters is [benefit]."

"Second, you get [Component 2]. [Description + benefit]."

"Third, [Component 3]. [Description + benefit]."

"Fourth, [Component 4]. [Description + benefit]."

[OPTIONAL: BONUSES]
"And because [reason — limited time / new launch / specific occasion], you also get:
- [Bonus 1] — [why it matters]
- [Bonus 2] — [why it matters]"
```

**Rules:**
- State the price UPFRONT. Transparency converts.
- Don't fake-stack value with inflated component prices.
- Each component: lead with the benefit, end with the feature.

---

### Step 5 — Handle objections (3–5 minutes)

Address their top 5–7 concerns BEFORE they raise them.

```
[FRAME THE OBJECTIONS SECTION]
"Now, before I show you how to get started, I want to address the questions I get most often."

[OBJECTION 1]
"Question 1: '[Their question — verbatim]'
Answer: [Direct, honest, 2–3 sentences]"

[OBJECTION 2]
"Question 2: '[Their question]'
Answer: [Direct]"

[OBJECTION 3]
"Question 3: '[Their question]'
Answer: [Direct]"

[OBJECTION 4 — usually price/payment]
"'How does pricing work?' — [Plain answer. Payment plan if available.]"

[OBJECTION 5 — usually time/results]
"'How long until I see results?' — [Honest expectation setting.]"

[OBJECTION 6 — usually fit]
"'Is this right for me if I [common situation]?' — [Direct yes/no answer.]"

[OBJECTION 7 — usually guarantee]
"'What if it doesn't work?' — [State the guarantee in plain language.]"
```

**Rules:**
- Use the actual top objections from the brief.
- Direct, honest answers — no spin.
- Don't dismiss any objection. Validate first.

---

### Step 6 — Make recommendations (1–2 minutes)

Tell them exactly what to do next.

```
[RECOMMENDATION FRAMEWORK]
"Here's what I recommend:

If you're [type 1 — e.g. 'just exploring'], [recommendation — e.g. 'check out the free training first'].

If you're [type 2 — e.g. 'ready to commit'], [recommendation — e.g. 'book a strategy call below'].

If you're [type 3 — e.g. 'a good fit but not sure'], [recommendation — e.g. 'reach out and we'll figure it out together']."
```

**Rules:**
- Help them self-segment.
- Make the right next step obvious for each type.

---

### Step 7 — Final CTA (30–60 seconds)

The single clear command.

```
[CALL TO ACTION]
"So here's what to do right now: [single specific action — click below, fill out the form, book the call].

[CTA BUTTON COPY: "Book My Strategy Call" / "Reserve My Seat" / "Get Instant Access"]

[REINFORCE]
"This will [specific outcome of clicking — e.g. 'open a booking page where you can pick a time that works for you']."

[OPTIONAL: SCARCITY]
"I'm currently taking [X] people this month. Once those are filled, the next intake is [date]."
```

**Rules:**
- One command. Not three.
- Tell them what happens after they click.
- Real scarcity only — never manufactured.

---

### Step 8 — Provide guarantee (30–60 seconds)

Reverse the risk.

```
[GUARANTEE]
"And here's my promise to you: [specific guarantee terms]. If [condition], I'll [refund / extend / replace / etc.]."

[PHILOSOPHY]
"I offer this because [reason — usually 'I only want to work with people who get results' or 'I stand behind my work']."
```

**Rules:**
- Real guarantee with real terms.
- Plain language, no fine print theatre.

---

### Step 9 — Close with vision (1–2 minutes)

Final emotional close. Paint the future.

```
[VISION PARAGRAPH]
"So imagine where you could be [timeframe] from now if you take action today.

[Sensory walkthrough — 2–3 paragraphs of what their life looks like once the transformation happens. Use 'you' throughout. Make it specific.]

That's the path I want to walk with you. The choice is yours."

[FINAL CTA]
"Click [button] now to get started. I'll see you on the other side."

[SIGN-OFF]
"This is [Name]. Thanks for watching."
```

**Rules:**
- Sensory, specific, future-paced.
- Use "you" as the actor in the scene.
- End with the CTA, not with the sign-off — leave them in motion.

## Speaking & writing rules for VSL specifically

### No parallel fragment triads

These sound robotic when read aloud. **Always merge into flowing sentences for spoken copy.**

❌ "More energy. Easier fat loss. Better results."

✅ "This gets you more energy, helps you lose fat way easier, and actually gives you results that stick."

### Sentence length

- Mix lengths. Short for impact. Longer for explanation.
- If you can't say a sentence in one breath, it's too long. Break it.

### Filler words to avoid

- "Look, here's the thing..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "Long story short..."
- "Essentially..."
- "Basically..."

### Read it out loud, twice

- Once at normal speed — fix anything that makes you stumble.
- Once at 1.5x speed (mimicking how viewers will watch) — fix anything that gets confusing under pace.

## Common VSL mistakes

| Mistake                                              | Fix                                                       |
|------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| 8-minute "intro" before getting to the point         | Hook in step 1 must include the *outcome* you deliver. State it in the first 30 seconds. |
| Hidden price                                         | State price in step 4. Transparency converts.             |
| Skipping objection handling                          | Top 5–7 objections from brief. Pre-empt them all.         |
| Multiple CTAs ("book a call OR download OR sign up") | One. Just one.                                            |
| Generic testimonials                                 | Specific named clients with numbers only.                 |
| Reading the script verbatim on camera                | Internalize the script. Hit the beats. Don't read.        |
| Parallel fragment triads in spoken delivery          | Merge into flowing sentences. Always.                     |
| Energy that drops in the back half                    | Energy curve should peak in step 9 (vision close).        |
| Closing with the sign-off, not the CTA               | Always end on motion — "Click below now."                 |
