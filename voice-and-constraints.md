# Voice & Constraints

The rules that separate professional direct response from amateur AI sludge.

---

## Tone

The default tone for all direct response copy:

- **Conversational authority.** Like explaining something to a smart friend over coffee.
- **Confident but not arrogant.** Saying *"this works"* is fine. Saying *"this is the only thing that works"* is bragging.
- **Helpful but not pushy.** Lead the reader. Don't drag them.
- **Direct but not harsh.** *"Most people fail because they skip step 2"* not *"You're failing because you're lazy."*

Think: a competent specialist who's done this 10,000 times and is sharing what works. Not a salesman. Not a guru.

---

## Sentence structure

- **Mix lengths.** Short, punchy sentences. Followed by longer, explanatory ones. The contrast is what creates rhythm.
- **Average target: 12–15 words per sentence.**
- **Single-sentence paragraphs land hardest.** Use them for emphasis. Sparingly.

### Example — mixed rhythm

> *Most fitness coaches will never crack $5k a month. Not because they don't have skills. Not because the market isn't there. Because they spend 90% of their time delivering and 0% on the part of the business that actually moves the needle: getting in front of new buyers.*

That's: short, short, short, longer with the payoff. Reads naturally because of the contrast.

---

## Vocabulary

- **Use power words sparingly.** *"Devastating", "ruthless", "shattered"* — these work once per page, not once per paragraph.
- **Prefer simple, concrete language over jargon.** Replace *"leverage synergies"* with *"use the connections you already have."*
- **No corporate stiffness or PR-speak.** Sounds robotic. Doesn't convert.

---

## The corporate filter test

If a sentence sounds like it came from a press release, rewrite it as if you're sending a 1-on-1 email to a friend.

| Press release version                                          | Conversational version                                            |
|----------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| "We leverage cutting-edge methodologies to deliver results."    | "Here's the system that's worked for 200+ coaches."              |
| "Our revolutionary platform unlocks unprecedented growth."      | "It's a stupid simple sequence — three emails, sent in order."   |
| "Industry-leading expertise drives transformative outcomes."    | "I've been in the trenches with people exactly like you for 8 years." |

The conversational versions convert. The press release versions don't.

## Forbidden phrases — never use these

These phrases are pattern-matched by readers as marketing fluff. The moment they appear, the reader starts skimming or unsubscribing.

### Banned words / phrases

```
game-changer       unlock              unleash
revolutionary      cutting-edge        seamless
synergy            innovative          paradigm shift
leverage (verb)    transformative      next-level
disrupt            empower             optimize (when overused)
turbocharge        skyrocket           harness
```

### Banned opening hooks

```
"In today's world..."
"Are you tired of..."
"Imagine if..."          (OK in body copy. Never as opener.)
"In a world where..."
"Have you ever wondered..."
"Did you know that..."
```

These openers signal "I'm about to sell you something" within the first 5 words. Reader bounces. Find a different way in:
- Lead with a specific stat.
- Lead with a story.
- Lead with a contrarian claim.
- Lead with the reader's own thought.

### Banned phrase patterns

- ❌ "Are you tired of [pain]?" → reader thinks: "yes, and now I'm tired of being asked."
- ❌ "What if I told you..." → too theatrical.
- ❌ "The truth is..." → implies you've been lying. Cut.
- ❌ "Look, here's the thing..." → filler. Cut.
- ❌ "At the end of the day..." → cliché. Cut.
- ❌ "Long story short..." → if it's long, shorten the story before the sentence.

## Grammar & style rules

### Active voice — always

| Passive (bad)                              | Active (good)                                  |
|--------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| "Results are delivered by our team."       | "Our team delivers results."                   |
| "The program was designed to..."           | "I designed this program to..."                |
| "Leads will be generated through..."        | "We generate leads through..."                  |

If you can put "by zombies" after the verb, it's passive. ("Results are delivered ~~by zombies~~.") Rewrite.

### Exclamation points

- **Maximum 1 per 100 words.**
- More than that and you sound desperate.
- Most direct response copy needs **zero** exclamation points.

### Hashtags

- **Never** in email subject lines or sales copy.
- Acceptable in social posts only.

### Pronouns

- **Always second-person.** Talk to *one* reader, even when sending to thousands.
- ❌ "We help businesses scale." (corporate)
- ✅ "Here's how you scale your business." (direct)

The exception: when you're telling a story about yourself or a client, first-person is fine. Just don't talk about your *company* in third person.

### No parallel fragment triads in spoken copy

This is one of the most common AI-isms and it sounds robotic when read aloud.

❌ Don't write (in VSLs, ads, or anything spoken):

> *"More energy. Easier fat loss. Better results."*

✅ Do write:

> *"This gets you more energy, helps you lose fat way easier, and actually gives you results that stick."*

Written-only copy (emails, sales pages, blog posts) is exempt — the visual rhythm of three short fragments works on the page. But the moment your copy gets recorded or read aloud, merge into flowing connected sentences.

## Layout rules

### Paragraphs

- **Maximum 3 sentences per paragraph.**
- **Maximum 4 lines per paragraph on mobile** (assume narrow screens).
- Single-sentence paragraphs land hardest. Use them for emphasis.

### Bullets

- Start each bullet with an **action verb or a benefit**.
- Maintain parallel structure across all bullets in a list.
- Don't use bullets to dump features — translate every feature to a benefit first.

✅ Good bullets:

> - Cut your sales-call no-show rate from 40% to under 10%
> - Convert 3x more leads from the same ad spend
> - Build a system you can hand off to a junior team in 6 months

❌ Bad bullets:

> - Sales call optimization
> - Better lead conversion
> - Team scalability

### Bold text

- **Maximum 10% of total body text.**
- Bold key benefits, deadlines, or specific numbers — not random emphasis.
- More than 10% bolded and the eye stops registering bold = important.

### Subheads

- **Every 150–200 words** in long-form copy.
- **Benefit-driven, not descriptive.**
- ❌ "About Our Features"
- ✅ "Cut Your Workload in Half — Without Hiring"

### White space

- Generous. Visual breathing room raises perceived value.
- Break up dense text blocks with line breaks, dividers, or images.

### Reading level

- **6th-grade.** Test with Hemingway Editor or similar.
- Anything above 8th-grade is too complex for direct response.

## The three voice tests

Run every piece of copy through these:

### 1. Read it out loud

If you stumble, your reader will stumble. If a sentence makes you take an awkward breath, it's too long. If a paragraph sounds robotic, it is.

### 2. The "smart friend over coffee" test

Would you actually say this sentence to a friend? Or would you only ever write it in marketing copy? If the answer is "only in marketing," rewrite it.

### 3. The replace-with-real-words test

Take any sentence with a forbidden word (game-changer, unlock, leverage). Rewrite it using only words a 10-year-old would use. Almost always shorter. Almost always more powerful.

> *"This game-changing methodology unlocks unprecedented results."*
>
> ↓
>
> *"This system gets you results most people never see."*

Same idea. Half the words. 10x the impact.

## Voice calibration by client

Every client has a slightly different voice. The toolkit's defaults are aggressive direct-response — adjust based on the brief.

| Client type                       | Voice adjustments                                              |
|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Aggressive direct-response coach** | Default toolkit voice. Direct, contrarian, pulls no punches. |
| **Premium/luxury brand**             | Less pain-agitation. More aspiration. Fewer urgency tactics. |
| **B2B SaaS / enterprise**            | Professional but not stiff. Specific numbers and ROI focus. Cut "you" pronouns slightly — "your team" feels right. |
| **Health & wellness**                | Gentler agitation. Lean on transformation, not fear. Watch FTC compliance language. |
| **Faith-based / mission-driven**     | Lead with values. Skip aggressive scarcity. Use community language. |

When the brief specifies `tone`, match it. When it doesn't, ask before defaulting.

## What "good" voice looks like — three quick examples

### Example 1 — sales page hero

> Most fitness coaches will hit a $5k/month ceiling and stay there for years.
>
> Not because they don't have skills. Not because the market isn't there. Because the time it takes to deliver to clients eats the time they'd need to find more clients.
>
> Here's the system that breaks the loop. It works without you needing to post more, network more, or hire anyone yet.

— Specific. Conversational. No banned words. Active voice. The reader sees themselves.

### Example 2 — VSL hook

> If you're a coach, consultant, or service provider stuck under $10k a month, you're going to want to see this.
>
> I've spent 8 years working with exactly your situation, and I can tell you why you're stuck — and what to do about it. It's not what most gurus tell you.

— Direct. Specific to the listener. Builds curiosity without theatre.

### Example 3 — ad

> 73% of fitness coaches make under $4k a month. The 27% who break through aren't doing more work — they're doing one thing differently.

— One sharp stat. One contrarian claim. Hooks the reader in under 3 seconds.

## When voice goes wrong — debugging

| Symptom                                          | Likely cause                              | Fix                                                          |
|--------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| "Sounds like AI"                                 | Hedge words, banned phrases, no specifics | Cut hedge words. Add concrete numbers. Use contractions.     |
| "Sounds too corporate"                            | Press release vocabulary                  | Apply the corporate filter test. Rewrite as 1-on-1 email.    |
| "Sounds too aggressive"                          | Over-agitation, too many "you must" framings | Soften with "most coaches in your seat..." instead of direct accusation. |
| "Doesn't sound like the client"                   | No voice samples in the brief             | Get 3 voice samples from the client. Re-write key sections.  |
| "Reads fine on screen but bad out loud"           | Parallel fragment triads, long sentences  | Merge fragments into flowing sentences. Break long sentences. |
| "Reader confused"                                 | Reading level too high, jargon            | Run Hemingway Editor. Aim for 6th-grade.                     |
